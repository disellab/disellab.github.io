Applying to the PhD program

Where to apply

Apply to the GW School of Engineering and Applied Science and choose Computer Science as the program. Admission is through the department, not the lab, so there is no separate lab application.

How to make sure we see your application

List Courtney Miller in the "Research Faculty of Interest" field. That is what routes your file to us. An email saying you applied does not guarantee a review, but the field does.

What we look for

Research ability first. Hands-on empirical work and prior publications matter most, in software engineering or anywhere else. You do not need prior experience with our specific topics; you need to have done research and be able to show it. Read the publications to judge fit, since the papers are the best description of what the lab does.

Can we meet before you apply

No. We cannot schedule fit meetings or pre-review applications or materials. Every applicant gets the same process, which is the application itself.

Application fee

A fee waiver is available. The current code is on Courtney Miller's FAQ, which is kept up to date.

Current and incoming GW students

If you are already a GW CS PhD student and want to collaborate or discuss advising, email with a short note on what you have worked on and what you want to work on. If you are a GW undergraduate or master's student, the lab is primarily recruiting PhD students in 2026; use the research interest form rather than email so we can reach you when a project opens.

Application materials you can borrow

Courtney's own statements and application packages are public: faculty job market materials, the PhD dissertation, the NSF GRFP application, and the CMU PhD application. They are on the application materials page, with links to PhD and faculty application guides.

What working here is like

We study the open source software supply chains that form our digital infrastructure and the sociotechnical disruptions they face, from Generative AI development tools defining software development to escalating supply chain sustainability and security threats. Our work is grounded in and guided by the needs of practitioners and uses rigorous multi-dimensional empirical mixed method approaches, combining human-centered qualitative techniques with large-scale data-driven statistical analysis. Students learn the full approach and own projects from design through publication.
